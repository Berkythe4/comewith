Blue Sky to Banked

A gig starts as something you want and ends as money in the account. There are four states in between, and the whole point of writing the early ones down is that a forecast built on hope is not a forecast.

The four states

01

Blue Sky

You want it. You do not have it. Nobody has agreed to anything.

counts at expected × confidence
02

Confirmed

It is happening. There is a date and an agreement, but no money has moved.

counts at the agreed fee
03

Accrued

Earned. You have played it or delivered it, and you are owed.

counts in full · shows as owed
04

Realized

Received. The money is in the account.

the only state that is cash

An event moves left to right, and the row never changes identity — the same event carries its history from wishful thinking to banked. That is what makes your hit rate measurable instead of anecdotal.

Two dials, not one

Stage is how real it is. Status is whether it is still alive.

These are separate on purpose and easy to conflate. Stage runs Blue Sky → Planning → Confirmed → Live → Wrapped → Reported. Status runs planning → announced → on sale → sold out → completed → cancelled.

A Blue Sky gig that dies is Status = cancelled. You do not delete it, and you do not move its stage backwards. It stays in the record as a thing you chased and did not get, which is the only way you ever learn what your conversion rate is.

Why the forecast discounts you

expected revenue  ×  confidence %  =  weighted forecast

Ten $1,000 gigs at 30% confidence is $3,000 of forecast, not $10,000. If you enter the full number and hope, the forecast tells you what you want to hear — and the whole reason to write down speculative work is to stop it doing that.

Set both on Edit event → Pipeline & expected money. Confidence is 0–100. A rough number beats a blank: blank is treated as zero and contributes nothing at all.

What reaches the P&L, and when

StateIn the forecastIn the P&LIs it cash

Accrued / Invoiced—Yes, as revenueNo

Nothing speculative ever touches the P&L

The P&L reads income and expense rows. A Blue Sky event has neither until it becomes real, so no amount of optimism in the pipeline can inflate your actuals. The two numbers answer different questions and must never be added together.

Cash reserve is stricter still: it moves only on Received. An invoice you are sure of is revenue, and it is not money.

The gap list

Upcoming events with no money on them

Any future event with no booked revenue, no accrual and no estimate is flagged. Left alone, an event with nothing on it stays invisible until it becomes a past event with nothing on it — which is too late to do anything about.

All 8 upcoming events currently sit on this list — RainbowTuTu, NYC Rave Girls, b2b Open Decks, JunXion, Dance Infusion #3, Hulaween, Come With #2 and the Toddler Rave Fundraiser. Ten minutes with expected revenue and confidence clears it.

Recipes

  1. Add a gig you want but do not have Create the event as normal, then Edit event → Pipeline & expected money: Stage Blue Sky, expected revenue, confidence. Give it your best-guess date — it needs one to land in the right forecast month.
  2. It got booked Same panel: Stage → Confirmed, and raise confidence to 100. Then record the fee on the Income tab against that event, status Accrued. The expected figure stops mattering the moment a real income row exists.
  3. It fell through Status → cancelled. Do not delete it. It drops out of the pipeline and stays in the history so you can see how many you chased per one you landed.
  4. You played it and you are owed Income tab → the fee, status Accrued (“agreed, not yet invoiced”) or Invoiced (“billed, waiting on payment”). Both count as revenue immediately; neither counts as cash. Filter Owed to us to see everything outstanding.
  5. The money arrived Set the row to Received. If the amount differs from what was agreed, enter what actually landed — the difference against the agreed figure is reported back to you rather than quietly absorbed.
  6. The event genuinely made nothing Say so explicitly with Confirm no revenue. “Earned nothing” and “nobody has recorded it yet” look identical in the data, and only one of them is a problem. Recording revenue later still works if you were wrong.
