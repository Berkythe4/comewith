The Photo Library

Every photo Come With holds lives in one library. There are two ways in depending on where the photo came from, one rule about where it can live, and one thing that is easy to get wrong: what is public.

Two ways in, one library

Event Hub → Photos

Photos from a show

Open the event, go to its Photos section, drag files onto the drop zone.

  • The event is filled in for you
  • Order them with ↑ ↓ — that order is the public gallery's order
  • Pick a photographer once and the rest of the shoot inherits it
Photos tab → ➕ Add photos

Everything else

Press shoots, portraits, gear, anything that did not come from a show.

  • You choose the subject — who or what the photos are of
  • Event is optional. Name the session instead, e.g. Press shoot — July 2026
  • Photographer and date are set for the whole batch before anything uploads

↓ both land in the same library ↓

The Photos tab shows everything from both doors together, and is where you search, filter, credit and publish. Filter by subject, by photographer, or by “No photographer credited” to find the gaps.

The one rule

A photo needs an event or a subject

At least one, enforced by the database. A photo with neither has no way of ever being found again, so it cannot be uploaded in the first place.

This is why a press shoot does not need a fake event. Making one would put a photo session in the events list, in the pipeline, and in the P&L next to real shows. Give it a subject and a shoot name instead.

Public is a decision, not a default

Nothing goes on the site until you tick “on site”

Uploads are private. The library is there for you to pick from; publishing is a separate, deliberate click, per photo.

But the storage bucket itself is public. “On site” controls whether the public gallery lists a photo — it does not make the file unreachable to someone holding the URL. That is fine for photographs. It is not fine for documents, contracts or anything with personal data: those go in Files, which is a private bucket.

Credit the photographer

Crediting is usually a condition of using someone's work, and doing it from memory is how it gets missed. Set the photographer and the credit writes itself everywhere.

To do thisDo this
Credit one photoPick the name in the dropdown on the photo's card. Saves immediately.
Credit a whole shootEvent Hub → Photos: pick the photographer at the top, then Credit the N uncredited or Credit all N.
Credit as you uploadPick the photographer before dropping files — it sticks to everything you add next.
Find what is missingPhotos tab → filter No photographer credited. Untagged photos also show a red no credit flag on the card.
Send someone their workView portfolio → — everything they have shot, grouped by event, with full-size downloads.
Publish a photo somewhereCopy the credit line from the portfolio view: Photo by Tori Mumtaz (@handle). Built once, so it reads the same everywhere.

If the credit should differ from the name

A studio name, or an agreed handle — set credit_line on the photo and it overrides the photographer's display name. Leave it empty and the name is used.

Things that take care of themselves

DuplicatesEvery file is fingerprinted by its contents, not its name, across the whole library. Re-dropping the same shoot is harmless — copies are skipped and counted, and two cameras both producing IMG_0001 are not confused for each other.
File sizeEach photo is resized in your browser before upload: a ~1600px version for downloads and a small thumbnail for grids. The galleries serve thumbnails.
iPhone photosHEIC is converted to JPEG automatically. Just drop them.
DeletingRemoves the image files too, and cannot be undone — unlike expenses, which are only ever soft-deleted.

Recipes

  1. Professional photos taken of you Photos tab → ➕ Add photos. Subject Keith, photographer Tori Mumtaz, leave Event empty, shoot name Press shoot — July 2026. Everything lands private; publish individual shots later if you ever want them on the site.
  2. Photos from a show you just played Event Hub → the event → Photos. Pick the photographer at the top first, then drop the whole folder. Order them, then tick “on site” on the ones the public gallery should carry.
  3. You inherited photos and do not know who shot them Upload them anyway. They show a red no credit flag and appear under the No photographer credited filter until someone says. That is the point — an unanswered question stays visible instead of disappearing.
  4. A photographer asks for their portfolio Photos tab → pick them → View portfolio →. Grouped by event, click any image for the full-size file, and the credit line is there to copy.
  5. You need a photo for a post Filter to the event or subject, then the ⬇ button on the card saves the full-size version. It downloads named after the credit, so the attribution travels with the file.
