Keith worked at **Maersk**, where he built budgets and managed forecast vs
actual on a real cadence — **weekly at times, usually twice monthly**. He is not
learning FP&A from this tool; he is expecting the tool to do what he already
knows how to do by hand.

Practical consequences for how to present financial work to him:

- He reads a **contribution / unit-economics** frame naturally. "What does one
  party contribute, and how many do we need" lands; a raw category-by-month
  expense dump does not.
- He explicitly de-prioritised **variance-to-commitment** ("meeting commitments,
  that's not as important") in favour of **continuously re-forecasting expected
  earnings**. Do not build ceremony around budget approval or lock-in.
- He thinks in **levers**: change the volume, see the answer move. A number he
  cannot push on is a report, and he did not ask for reports.
- Cadence should be offered, never enforced — a round gets frozen when he says
  so, not on a schedule.
